ISL AI Cup

A competition for students at Independent School League schools. Teams spend the spring building something real with AI, and the best projects present live at finals in May.

The idea

Two-person teams pick something they care about and build it. That could be a tool for a local nonprofit, a research model, an app, a game, or the start of a company. You don't need to know how to code, and you can use any AI tool you want. The one rule is that it has to work. Ideas and slide decks don't count.

How it runs

January
Kickoff. Teams sign up with a one-paragraph idea.
Feb to April
Teams build on their own schedule. Once a month there's an optional session, either at a coalition company or online, and every session is recorded. Mentors are available for teams that want them.
March
One check-in: a short progress update and a screenshot. Teams that miss it drop out.
Early May
Submissions close. Each team sends a working project, a 2–3 minute demo video, and a short write-up that explains how they used AI.
Late May
Finals. The top 8 to 10 teams present live to judges from coalition companies.
  • Projects have to be new, or meaningfully extended during the Cup, so students with old polished projects don't win by default.
  • Each team has a faculty advisor at its school, and every student needs a signed parent consent form.

Awards

Every finalist is judged on three questions: does it work, does it matter to someone, and how far did the team push past what they could do before. Then awards go out by category.

Grand PrizeThe strongest project overall.
Most ImpactThe project that does the most good for real people, a school, a nonprofit or a community.
Most TechnicalThe hardest engineering problem, solved well.
Best First BuildFor a team that had never built anything like this before the Cup.

Sponsors can also put their name on a category or add one of their own.

Where the coalition fits

Hackachusetts already brings college students from across the state together to build, with the finals pitched at WHOOP. The Cup could be the high school version, run over a semester so students can fit it around school. It can run under the coalition's name, with member companies hosting sessions, mentoring and judging, and finals held at a member's office. We're also happy to help at coalition events wherever students would be useful.

What would help

Any one of these is a real help. We don't expect any single company to cover everything.

  • Prize money. We're aiming for about $10,000 in total, pooled across all sponsors.
  • Credits or free access to your tools for the teams.
  • Hosts or speakers for the monthly sessions.
  • Mentors who can give a team an hour or two.
  • Judges for finals.
  • A place to hold finals.

Where it stands

Early. No schools or sponsors have signed on yet. We're talking to companies first, because a program that's already funded is much easier to bring to ISL schools. If year one goes well, we want to open it to more Massachusetts schools, including public schools, in 2027–28.
