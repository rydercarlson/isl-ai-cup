Proposal

ISL AI Cup

A spring-long AI build competition for high school students at Independent School League schools. We're looking for companies to help fund it, mentor teams and judge the finals.

Teams
2 students each
Runs
January to May 2027
Open to
Any ISL student
Finals
Late May, live

The idea

Teams of two pick a problem they care about and build something that solves it. It could be a tool for a local nonprofit, a research model, an app, a game, or the start of a company. No coding experience is needed, and teams can use any AI tools they like. What matters is that it works by the end. Ideas and slide decks don't count.

How it runs

January
Kickoff. Teams sign up with a one-paragraph idea.
Feb to April
Teams build on their own schedule. Once a month there's an optional session, in person at a sponsor's office or online, and each one is recorded. Teams can ask for a mentor.
March
One check-in: a short progress update and a screenshot. Teams that skip it are out.
Early May
Submissions close. Each team sends in a working project, a 2–3 minute demo video, and a short write-up on how they used AI.
Late May
Finals. The top 8 to 10 teams present live to judges from sponsor companies.
  • Projects must be started or substantially extended during the Cup, so nobody wins with something they finished last year.
  • Every team has a faculty advisor at its own school, and every student needs a signed parent consent form.

Awards

Judges score each finalist on three questions. Does it work? Does it matter to someone? How far did the team get past what they could do before? Prizes go out in four categories.

Grand PrizeThe strongest project overall.
Most ImpactThe project that does the most good for real people, a school, a nonprofit or a community.
Most TechnicalThe hardest engineering problem, solved well.
Best First BuildFor a team building something like this for the first time.

Sponsors can put their name on a category or create their own.

Where the coalition fits

The coalition helped run Hackachusetts this year for college students, with finals pitched at WHOOP. The Cup would be the high school version, spread over a semester so it fits around school. It could run under the coalition's name, with member companies hosting sessions, mentoring and judging, and finals at a member's office. We're also glad to send students to help at coalition events.

What would help

Each of these helps on its own. No single company needs to cover everything.

  • Prize money: about $10,000 in total, pooled across all sponsors.
  • Credits or free access to your tools for the teams.
  • Hosts or speakers for the monthly sessions.
  • Mentors who can give a team an hour or two.
  • Judges for finals.
  • A place to hold finals.

Where it stands

It's early. No sponsors or schools have committed yet. We're starting with companies because a funded program is much easier to bring to ISL schools. The plan is to run year one in the ISL in spring 2027, then open it to more Massachusetts schools, including public schools, in 2027–28.
